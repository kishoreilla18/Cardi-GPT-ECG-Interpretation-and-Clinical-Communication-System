Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB)

Left Bundle Branch Block occurs when there is a delay or blockage in the electrical impulses traveling through the left bundle branch of the heart’s conduction system. This condition alters the normal sequence of ventricular activation, causing the left ventricle to contract later than the right ventricle. On an ECG, it typically shows a widened QRS complex and distinct waveform patterns, which can help in diagnosis.

LBBB can be associated with underlying heart diseases such as hypertension, coronary artery disease, or cardiomyopathy. While some patients may be asymptomatic, others may experience chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting spells. Early detection is important as it may indicate structural heart problems that need further evaluation and management.

{% elif predicted_result == "Normal" %}

Normal

A normal ECG indicates that the heart’s electrical activity is functioning within healthy limits. The P wave, QRS complex, and T wave appear in their usual shape, size, and timing, representing a regular rhythm and normal conduction through the atria and ventricles. This shows that the heart is pumping efficiently and delivering oxygen-rich blood throughout the body.

Even though a normal ECG suggests good heart health at the time of recording, it does not completely rule out all heart conditions. Some issues may not be present during the test or may require additional investigations. However, it provides reassurance and serves as a baseline for comparing future readings.

{% elif predicted_result == "Premature Atrial Contraction" %}

Premature Atrial Contraction (PAC)

Premature Atrial Contractions are early heartbeats originating from the atria before the normal heartbeat occurs. These extra beats disrupt the heart’s rhythm temporarily and are often seen as irregularities on the ECG. PACs may occur occasionally in healthy individuals or may be linked to stress, caffeine, alcohol, or underlying heart conditions.

Most PACs are harmless and do not require treatment, especially if they occur infrequently. However, frequent PACs may be associated with atrial fibrillation or other arrhythmias. Identifying PACs on an ECG helps in monitoring and preventing complications in patients with risk factors.

{% elif predicted_result == "Premature Ventricular Contractions" %}

Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs)

Premature Ventricular Contractions are extra heartbeats that begin in the ventricles and occur earlier than the next expected heartbeat. On an ECG, they appear as wide and abnormal QRS complexes. PVCs can be occasional and benign, especially in healthy individuals, or frequent and linked to heart disease, electrolyte imbalance, or medication effects.

Some patients may feel PVCs as skipped beats, palpitations, or chest discomfort. While occasional PVCs are generally not dangerous, frequent PVCs may increase the risk of arrhythmias or weakened heart function. Identifying PVCs on ECG is important to determine whether further tests or treatment are needed.

{% elif predicted_result == "Right Bundle Branch Block" %}

Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB)

Right Bundle Branch Block occurs when the electrical conduction is slowed or blocked along the right bundle branch, delaying the activation of the right ventricle. On an ECG, this condition typically shows as a widened QRS complex with characteristic “rabbit ear” patterns in certain leads.

RBBB can be seen in both healthy individuals and in association with heart disease such as congenital heart defects, pulmonary embolism, or myocardial infarction. While it may not always cause symptoms, its presence can indicate underlying cardiac or pulmonary issues that should be evaluated further.

{% elif predicted_result == "Ventricular Fibrillation" %}

Ventricular Fibrillation (VFib)

Ventricular Fibrillation is a life-threatening arrhythmia where the ventricles quiver instead of contracting effectively, leading to no effective blood circulation. On an ECG, VFib appears as chaotic, irregular electrical activity without recognizable P waves, QRS complexes, or T waves. This condition requires immediate medical attention.

Without prompt treatment, usually defibrillation and advanced cardiac life support, VFib leads to sudden cardiac arrest and death. Identifying this condition quickly is critical for survival. Continuous monitoring and preventive treatment may be required for individuals at risk of recurrent VFib.
